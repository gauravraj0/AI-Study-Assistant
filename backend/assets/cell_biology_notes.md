# Cell Biology Basics — Study Notes

## The Cell as the Unit of Life

The cell is the smallest unit of life. Every living organism is made of one or more cells. A cell can carry out all the processes needed for life, such as metabolism, growth, and reproduction. Most organisms are multicellular, meaning they are made of many specialized cells working together.

## Cell Structure

The cell membrane is a lipid bilayer that controls what enters and leaves the cell. The nucleus contains the cell's DNA and controls the cell's activities. The cytoplasm is a gel-like substance that fills the cell and holds the organelles in place. Mitochondria are the powerhouse of the cell because they generate most of the cell's supply of ATP through cellular respiration. Ribosomes are the sites of protein synthesis inside the cell. The endoplasmic reticulum is a network of membranes involved in protein and lipid transport. The Golgi apparatus modifies, packages, and ships proteins to their destinations. Lysosomes contain digestive enzymes that break down waste materials and cellular debris.

## Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

Prokaryotic cells are smaller and lack a membrane-bound nucleus. Their DNA floats freely in the cytoplasm. Bacteria and archaea are prokaryotes. Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane. Animal and plant cells are eukaryotic. Eukaryotic cells also contain membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria and the Golgi apparatus.

## Plant Cells vs Animal Cells

Plant cells have a rigid cell wall made of cellulose outside the cell membrane. Animal cells do not have a cell wall. Plant cells contain chloroplasts, which capture light energy for photosynthesis. Both plant and animal cells contain mitochondria, but plant cells store excess energy in a large central vacuole. Animal cells may contain small vacuoles and a centriole for cell division.

## Cell Division

Mitosis is the process by which a cell divides to produce two genetically identical daughter cells. Mitosis has four stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, and telophase. During prophase, chromosomes condense and the nuclear membrane breaks down. During metaphase, chromosomes line up at the cell equator. During anaphase, sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles. During telophase, two new nuclei form. Cytokinesis is the division of the cytoplasm that completes cell division. Meiosis is a special type of cell division that produces gametes with half the number of chromosomes. Meiosis involves two rounds of division and creates genetic variation through crossing over and independent assortment.

## Transport Across the Membrane

Diffusion is the movement of particles from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration. Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane. Active transport moves substances against their concentration gradient using energy from ATP. Facilitated diffusion uses carrier proteins to move specific molecules down their concentration gradient. Endocytosis is the process where the cell takes in materials by wrapping its membrane around them. Exocytosis is the process where the cell expels materials by fusing vesicles with the membrane.
